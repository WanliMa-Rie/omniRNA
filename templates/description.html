OmniRNA: A Discovery Challenge for RNA Foundation Models

RNA plays a central role in molecular biology, but it remains unclear how sequence and structure encode RNA function and fate. OmniRNA is an ECML/PKDD discovery challenge designed to evaluate whether foundation models can learn general and biologically meaningful RNA representations.

The challenge is organized around three pillars of RNA biology: mutational fitness, RNA structure, and evolution. Participants are asked to build models that can predict the effects of genetic variants on RNA fitness, infer secondary and tertiary structure from sequence, and identify evolutionary signals that preserve function across divergence.

Tasks
  • RNA Fitness Prediction: predict experimentally measured fitness effects for RNA sequence variants.
  • RNA Structure Prediction: recover RNA secondary and tertiary structure from sequence.
  • RNA Evolutionary Reasoning: solve quartet phylogeny classification and covariation detection tasks.
Data Release

For each task, we provide training, validation, and development-test splits. A separate hidden final-test split is used for official ranking. Data, starter code, evaluation scripts, and submission templates are provided through the challenge repository and platform.

Why This Challenge Matters

Strong RNA models should generalize beyond narrow benchmarks. OmniRNA provides a standard and reproducible evaluation setting for RNA variant effect prediction, structure recovery, and evolutionary reasoning, with direct relevance to RNA therapeutics, synthetic biology, variant interpretation, and comparative genomics.
